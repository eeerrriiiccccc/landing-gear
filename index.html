What I built

  • Engineered a 3-legged retractable landing gear system from scratch for a Ø500 mm × 3 m rocket in SolidWorks. I owned the full design cycle: concept, part modeling, mated assembly, and engineering drawings.
  • Integrated a spring-damper shock absorber into each landing leg to dissipate touchdown impact energy and protect the airframe's structural integrity.

Design highlights

01

Folding three-segment legs

Each leg is an upper arm, a ribbed mid-leg, and a bottom leg. They hinge from a body-mounted connector, so the legs fold flush against the airframe for flight and swing out for landing.

02

Shock absorber in every leg

An 800 mm, Ø100 mm shock absorber sub-assembly (housing, cap, and a swept helical spring) runs along each leg and compresses on touchdown to soak up the impact load before it reaches the rocket body.

03

Mechanism modeled with mates

I constrained the assembly with concentric, symmetric, limit-distance, rack-and-pinion, and linear-coupler mates. Deployment and shock travel move realistically in SolidWorks instead of sitting as frozen geometry.

04

Built to be manufactured

The connectors use standard counterbored M5 hex-bolt holes from the Hole Wizard, the legs use thin-wall extrusions and ribs to save weight, and the parts are documented in engineering drawings.
